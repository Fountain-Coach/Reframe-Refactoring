# Beat Movement and the Uncertainty Overlay

> Chapter summary: A beat is a grounded dramatic movement. An uncertainty question may inhabit that beat, continue
> through it, change within it, or close because of it, but uncertainty is not the admission test for the beat. The
> Score's beat spine therefore survives resolved questions, while UncertaintyScoreKit projects question movement
> over that spine. Storify, persistence, projections, image participants, and scenarios must preserve both facts.

## Purpose

Chapter 28 correctly rejected atoms as beats, but made an open question the exclusive beat identity. That collapses
resolved dramatic material: a meeting, reversal, revelation, decision, or consequence disappears when the reading
does not leave a question open at the end. Pyramus and Thisbe exposed the defect. The story has clear movements even
where the reading treats the causal outcome as settled.

## Governing rules

1. **A beat is a grounded dramatic movement.** It may be an action, encounter, escalation, reversal, revelation,
   decision, consequence, or other source-grounded change. It is not a window, atom, or model-generated decorative
   heading.

2. **Uncertainty is an overlay, not a beat gate.** A beat may carry zero, one, or several uncertainty questions. A
   missing question never authorizes deleting the grounded beat; an uncertainty question never authorizes inventing
   a beat without source movement.

3. **The two movements remain distinct and related.** The beat records `dramaticMovement`; the uncertainty map records
   `questionMovement`: raised, carried, transformed, answered-by-the-text, deferred, or still-open-at-end. “Resolved”
   is a state of a question, never proof that the beat did not exist.

4. **Every semantic field keeps its evidence.** Beat movement and each question movement carry source anchors,
   provenance, the reading/lens identity, and the operation version. Absence of an anchor is preserved as unknown; no
   span is borrowed merely to make an illustration or score mark look complete.

5. **The Score spine is beat-authoritative.** Participants, including illustrations, attach to grounded beats. The
   participant may consume the beat movement alone or the movement plus its uncertainty overlay. It must not attach to
   an uncertainty note as if the note were the beat.

6. **UncertaintyScoreKit projects, it does not redefine.** Its lanes and notes may show question lifecycle and
   resolution over the beat spine. It must never return a zero beat count merely because its question lanes are empty.

7. **Storify must emit both contracts.** The semantic reading may return structural ordering/movement and optional
   uncertainties. Persistence, reconstruction, carry-forward, and projections must preserve both, including legacy
   and anchored question representations.

8. **Illustration eligibility is semantic, not numeric.** A beat is illustratable when it has grounded movement and
   admissible source evidence. A question overlay enriches the prompt when present; image generation must not require
   an uncertainty question unless a scenario explicitly targets uncertainty itself.

9. **Scenario acceptance names the intended shape.** A structural illustration scenario requires a persisted beat
   with movement and evidence. An uncertainty-led scenario additionally requires a question overlay and its lifecycle
   state. Neither scenario may pass from an image receipt alone.

## Naming

Use **dramatic movement** for what changes in the beat. Use **question movement** for the lifecycle of a question over
that beat. Use **resolution state** only for the question lifecycle (`answered`, `transformed`, `deferred`, or
`still-open-at-end`); never use it as a synonym for beat existence.

## Acceptance

- A resolved myth still produces grounded beats from its dramatic movements.
- A beat with no uncertainty is visible and remains eligible for a movement-based projection.
- A beat with uncertainty exposes the question and its question movement without replacing the beat.
- Store readback contains both beat movement and question-overlay evidence; reconstruction preserves structured and
  legacy questions.
- UncertaintyScoreKit, Score, and image projection agree on beat identity and source provenance.
- The Teatro myth scenario cannot reach image generation without a grounded beat; it requires uncertainty only when
  the scenario declares uncertainty as its target.

## Governing sentence

Reframe shall treat the beat as grounded dramatic movement and uncertainty as the question's movement across it:
related, jointly evidenced, and never substituted for one another.
